## Pass 1 — Thesis and vocabulary

- In one paragraph, what problem does the paper claim current agent systems fail to solve?
  - The paper, using the proposed architecture, attempts to combine heirarchical goal decomposition with persistent objectives, an evolving identity that adapts without needing retraining, simulative planning through an internal world model alongside reactive action, self regulation of when and how to deeply deliberate via a learned configurator, and self-directed learning from both real and simulated experience. The current agent systems are limited to the capabilities given via the software harness (exogenously), and the frozen checkpoint powering the LLM that is called within the harness for inference (until the next manual retraining-deployment cycle). Therefore, current agentic systems cannot solve the problem of scaling intelligence with an ever changing world, as it would be nearly impossible to enumerate every possible behavior through tools, prompts, or skills. A single agentive architecture aims to solve this without requiring manual updates to the harness and model.
  - Updated: The paper argues that contemporary agentic systems externalize improtant control functions including goal management, planning regulation, memory, and learning into engineered scaffolding around relatively static models. It proposes GIC as an architecture in which persistent goals, identity evolution, simulative planning, action, self-regulation, and learning are presented as interacting learned processes. The paper offers formal arguments and partial prototypes for selected mechanisms, but does not yet demonstrate complete architecture, persistent autonomous learning, or superiority over all external orchestration approaches.
- Define `agentic`, `agentive`, agent model, world model, goal, identity, configurator, planner, actor, and learning process in your own words.
  - **Agentic:** systems that complete tasks autonomously through orchestrated tools and workflows, relying on engineered software built around a given reasoning model. (section 1: introduction)
  - **Agentive:** systems that derive their capabilities endogenously and can self regulate, learn continuously, and decompose goals over long time horizons in a constantly changing world. (section 1: introduction)
  - **agent model**: a machine learning model capable of generating a broad range of actions iwth the flexibility, adaptability, and autonomy associated with humans and/or other animals, and of learning autonomously and perpetually. a reasoning model that generates real-world actions based on its goals and identity. (Section 1: introduction). formally the learned realization of the agent policy factor, it need not possess every human like capacity.
  - **world model:** a learned approximation of the universe in which the agent model is deployed, capable of predicting what may happen over time. (section 2.1). Does not have direct access to the true world state. under partial observability, it predicts future belief states from current beliefs and proposed actions.
  - **goal:** desired outcome guiding decision making over time (section 2.2)
  - i**dentity:** a collection of persistent properties taht influence decision-making across time, such as capabilities, constraints, affordances, and relationships with other entities (section 2.3)
  - **planner:** a form of system II reasoning called simulative reasoning in the paper wherein the agent selects actions that account for future consequences through deliberation with a world model that has direct access to the world state. The agent receives observations and infers beliefs representing its best estimate of the world. The world model predicts the next belief state given a proposed action. The world model is a learned realization of the universe and can operate in belief space, distinct from the agent that is querying it. The world model simulates sequences of actions and their predicted consequences, and the agent approximates optimal behavior without access to true environment dynamics. The optimal planner policy selects action sequences that maximize expected goal progress under simulated state transitions which are conditioned on the agent's current subgoal and identity. This simulative reasoning enables the agent to propose candidate actions, predict the consequences through the world model, and ~~s~~elect the consequence that maximizes expected long term progress. (section 2.4) The planner proposes candidates, queries the world model, obtains critic evaluations, and selects a plan.
  - **actor:** Given the selected plan based on deliberation by the planner, actions are selected and executed by an actor, responsible for handling fine-grained reactive execution. The reactive component captures execution patterns that are difficult to encode in structured plans and enables fast response when deliberation is unnecessary. Unlike externally imposed procedures seen in agentic systems, planning and action are an internal computational process, wherein the agent forms, revises, and acts on plans as a result of its own decision making. (section 2.4). The actor generates an action, an external runtime still executes tool calls or physical actions.
  - configurator: beyond the question of which action to take, long horizon planning poses the question of how a decision should be made. While some decisions are handled by direct policy execution, others benefit from extended deliberation and replanning. Such "meta-decisions" encompass whether to pursue or abandon a goal, whether to act or refrain from action, and how to prioritize competing objectives, extending beyond computational resource allocation to behavioral and normative dimensions. The capacity to control these internal modes of operation is considered self-regulation, which is modeled through a configurator, governing the agent's decision mode at each step by outputting a regulation variable, thus expressing whether to act directly, continue executing an existing plan, invoke additional planning, or revise goals. The configurator may extend beyond inference time to govern the agent's own learning process: when to act in the environment, when to retreat into simulation for practice, when to update the world model, and when to revise the self model (aka learning). (section 2.5)
  - identity evolution and weight learning are different processes. identity can change as persistent state without changing model parameters.
  - learning process: the process (denoted as lambda) that outputs the next parameter (theta t+1) given current parameters (theta t) and real and simulated experiences (D mu and D f). The agent is able ot update its parameters basesd on deployment experience as well as learn from simulated experience where it generates hypothetical trajectories through its world model (f) and trains on them without real world interaction. The world model improves by reducing prediciton error against observed transitions, while the agent's decision making components (theta) improve through goal directed feedback. This separation is noted as important. Learning is governed by the configurator as part of the agent's own policy,and the self model (identity) may also be updated as discussed earlier as a fast improvement procedure without full retraining. This is meant to be a continuous and autonomous perpetual process that augments external interaction with internal world model simulations and is self governed as part of its ongoing decision making (section 2.6). The learning process (lambda) is an abstraction, not a specified optimizer or demonstrated autonomous training subsystem.
- Which definitions are operational, and which are philosophical or normative?
  - Operational: linguistic and symbolic reasoning (reading, writing, coding), physical and spatial competence (navigation, manipulation), social understanding (coordinating and competing with other systems). observation, belief state, persistent goal, current subgoal, identity variable, configurator decision, plan, action, world-model transition prediction, critic value, and parameter update.
  - Philosophical: curiosity, self reflection, goal formation. "genuine agency", "internalized agency", "endogenous intelligence", the claim that external scaffolding is categorically less agentive.
  - Mixed: identity, self regulation, persistent goals. each has a formal variable, but the paper also gives it a broader interpretation about autonomy.
  - Critique: Could two systems implement the same conditional behavior while one places the mechanism inside a checkpoint and the other palces it in external typed software? If yes, then internalization needs a measurable operational definition before it can support a scientific comparison. Thought: is this akin to instinctive reactive behavior as opposed to planning or retraining? or would planning with the correct outcome be considered internalized, whereas an operational failure and human intervention would be a sign of lack of internalization?
- Which parts appear substrate-neutral, digital-specific, or embodiment-dependent?
  - substrate-neutral: the probablistic factorization, goal decomposition, identity state configurator, planner, actor, critic, and separation of agent and world model objectives
  - digital-specific in current evidence: language specific belief states, browser/tool actions, discrete turns, textual future-state predictions, and the companion prototypes
  - embodiment-dependent: multimodal perception, proprioception, continuous action, physical dynamics, hard real-time control, sim-to-real transfer, and physical safety supervision
  - the architecture is nominally substrate-neutral, the empirical evidence is substantially more narrow.

### Pass 3 synthesis

**Status:** Reconciled as a provisional implementation-contract exercise. These contracts include GIC Lab recommendations beyond what the paper specifies and do not constitute implementation evidence.

The component decomposition alone is not a build specification. Each factor requires typed inputs and outputs, explicit ownership of persistent state, a component-appropriate training signal, observable failure criteria, and documented implementation choices. Several cross-cutting boundaries are especially important:

- Beliefs, predictions, value judgments, plans, actions, and execution results must remain distinguishable so that failures can be attributed to the responsible component.
- Terminal-goal authority, permissions, hard safety constraints, evaluation roots, and promotion authority must not be silently mutable through ordinary identity or policy learning.
- The world model predicts consequences using prediction-only objectives; the critic evaluates those consequences; the planner proposes and selects trajectories; and the actor converts the selected decision into a typed action intent.
- Uncertainty and provenance must propagate across component boundaries rather than being reduced to unsupported narrative confidence.
- Learning produces versioned candidates. Independent evaluation, promotion, canarying, and rollback are separate control-plane operations.
- The contracts identify plausible implementations and tests, not claims that the paper has empirically validated a complete GIC system.

### Pass 4 synthesis

**Status:** Reconciled as a provisional falsification analysis; conversion of selected claims into preregistered experiment protocols remains a later decision.

- H-GIC-005, H-GIC-007, H-GIC-009, and H-GIC-011 contain conditional mathematical statements. Their strict falsifiers are counterexamples that satisfy every formal assumption; empirical failures outside those assumptions bear on applicability, not mathematical validity.
- Broad architectural claims using “can” are existential and resist finite falsification. They become scientific only after the implementation class, task distribution, resource budget, conformance criteria, and minimum useful effect are fixed in advance.
- Comparative claims require the strongest plausible external alternative, not only a reactive or ablated baseline. A typed external harness and a matched monolithic policy are recurring controls.
- Future-state text counts as a world-model prediction only when it is action-conditioned, timestamped, scored against later observation, calibrated, and causally relevant to action selection. Plausible prose alone is insufficient.
- Apparent benefits can be explained by extra tokens, model calls, candidate count, teacher quality, filtering, RL, evaluator coupling, state bandwidth, or unequal total capacity unless those variables are matched or separately reported.
- The safety-convergence claim H-GIC-014 is not presently falsifiable as written because harm, sufficient training, and convergence are undefined. Its dependence on a correct, fixed, exogenous terminal goal excludes goal misspecification from the claimed component-improvement pathway and must be explicit in any test.
- Negative findings are informative when the conformance and resource contracts hold. A failed implementation without those controls is evidence about that implementation, not the architecture in general.
